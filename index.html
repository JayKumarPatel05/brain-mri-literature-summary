PhD Committee Literature Matrix

Tabulated Summary of Research Papers

A committee-facing GitHub Pages summary organized around three proposed research concentrations: deep learning for medical imaging, edge AI / real-time inference, and semi-supervised learning. The table emphasizes not only methodology and results, but also limitations, research relevance, and critical questions for the proposed brain MRI segmentation direction. This revised survey-only version also preserves a fourth supporting area for graph-based, benchmark dataset, and evaluation-rigor papers from the verified survey batches.

Rows: 25 survey-bank papers Primary domain: Brain MRI segmentation Survey batches represented: 1–7 Updated: May 31, 2026

Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning Foundations

Foundational CNN, U-Net, 3D U-Net, V-Net, DeepMedic, Attention U-Net, UNet++, nnU-Net, and brain-tumor-specific U-Net variants.

Area 2 — Transformer and CNN–Transformer Brain MRI Segmentation

TransBTS, UNETR, nnFormer, and clinical-knowledge-guided hybrid transformer models for multimodal brain MRI segmentation.

Area 3 — Self-/Semi-Supervised Limited-Label Learning

Self-supervised transformer pretraining, uncertainty-aware teacher–student learning, shape-aware SSL, dual-task consistency, cross teaching, and mutual consistency.

Area 4 — Graph-Based, Dataset, Benchmark, and Evaluation Foundations

Graph attention, ViG-UNet, BraTS, UCSF-BMSR, BraTS-METS, and Metrics Reloaded as anchors for relational modeling and validation rigor.

1Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsFoundational CNN segmentationRonneberger, O., Fischer, P., & Brox, T. (2015). U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation. MICCAI.Biomedical segmentation with limited annotated data and need for precise localization.Symmetric encoder–decoder CNN with skip connections and strong augmentation.ISBI biomedical segmentation tasks; demonstrated strong performance from limited labels.Established the U-shaped encoder–decoder as the dominant template for biomedical segmentation.Simple, extensible, data-efficient, precise boundary recovery through skip connections.Originally 2D; limited long-range context and volumetric awareness.Baseline architecture from which most brain MRI segmentation methods in the survey evolve.How much of later progress comes from architecture versus preprocessing/training protocol?
2Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsVolumetric CNN segmentationÇiçek, Ö., Abdulkadir, A., Lienkamp, S. S., Brox, T., & Ronneberger, O. (2016). 3D U-Net: Learning Dense Volumetric Segmentation from Sparse Annotation. MICCAI.Dense volumetric segmentation when only sparse slice annotations may be available.Extends U-Net to 3D convolutions and performs dense prediction from volumetric context.Volumetric biological datasets; trained end-to-end with 3D elastic augmentation.Showed that 3D U-Net can learn dense volumetric segmentation from sparse annotation.Captures inter-slice anatomy; foundational for 3D MRI segmentation.High memory cost and dependence on patch size/annotation quality.Direct foundation for brain MRI tumor/lesion segmentation pipelines.Can sparse-label strategies reduce the annotation burden in brain MRI segmentation?
3Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsVolumetric fully convolutional segmentationMilletari, F., Navab, N., & Ahmadi, S.-A. (2016). V-Net: Fully Convolutional Neural Networks for Volumetric Medical Image Segmentation. 3DV.Whole-volume 3D medical segmentation under foreground/background imbalance.Volumetric FCN with residual blocks and Dice-based objective function.MRI prostate volumes; demonstrated efficient volumetric segmentation.Popularized Dice loss for highly imbalanced volumetric segmentation tasks.Directly optimizes overlap; suitable for small structures and lesion segmentation.Computationally expensive and originally evaluated outside brain tumor MRI.Supports the survey discussion of Dice-based training for brain tumor subregions.Does Dice optimization alone adequately handle small enhancing tumor regions?
4Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsBrain lesion CNN segmentationKamnitsas, K., Ledig, C., Newcombe, V. F. J., et al. (2017). Efficient Multi-Scale 3D CNN with Fully Connected CRF for Accurate Brain Lesion Segmentation. Medical Image Analysis.Accurate segmentation of heterogeneous brain lesions in multimodal MRI.Dual-pathway multi-scale 3D CNN with dense training and fully connected CRF post-processing.Brain lesion tasks including traumatic brain injury, brain tumors/BRATS, and ischemic stroke/ISLES.Reported top-ranking performance on public BRATS 2015 and ISLES 2015 benchmarks.Brain-specific, multi-scale, explicitly combines local and contextual features.Patch-based CNN and CRF post-processing increase pipeline complexity.Important bridge from generic CNNs to dedicated brain-lesion segmentation systems.How much post-processing should remain in modern end-to-end brain MRI models?
5Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsAttention-based U-NetOktay, O., Schlemper, J., Le Folgoc, L., et al. (2018). Attention U-Net: Learning Where to Look for the Pancreas.Need to suppress irrelevant regions and localize variable anatomical targets.Attention gates integrated into U-Net skip pathways to focus on salient spatial regions.Large abdominal CT segmentation datasets; compared against U-Net variants.Improved sensitivity and segmentation accuracy while adding limited overhead.Interpretable attention mechanism; easily inserted into U-Net-style models.Originally not brain MRI; attention maps do not guarantee clinical explanation.Motivates attention modules for tumor boundary and lesion-region focus.Can attention improve small-lesion detection without increasing false positives?
6Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsNested U-Net architectureZhou, Z., Siddiquee, M. M. R., Tajbakhsh, N., & Liang, J. (2018). UNet++: A Nested U-Net Architecture for Medical Image Segmentation. DLMIA/MICCAI Workshop.Semantic gap between encoder and decoder feature maps in U-Net.Nested dense skip pathways with deep supervision across encoder–decoder levels.Multiple medical segmentation datasets including chest nodules, nuclei, liver, and polyps.Reported average IoU gains over U-Net and wide U-Net baselines.Improves feature fusion and multi-scale refinement.More parameters and nested paths increase training/inference complexity.Important U-Net variant for discussing skip-connection refinement in brain MRI.Does added skip-path complexity translate to robust gains under MRI domain shift?
7Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsSelf-configuring segmentation baselineIsensee, F., Jaeger, P. F., Kohl, S. A. A., Petersen, J., & Maier-Hein, K. H. (2021). nnU-Net: A Self-configuring Method for Deep Learning-based Biomedical Image Segmentation. Nature Methods.Lack of robust, reproducible configuration across medical segmentation datasets.Self-configuring pipeline for preprocessing, architecture, training, post-processing, and ensemble decisions.Validated across many public biomedical segmentation tasks/challenges.Became a dominant baseline by showing training protocol can outperform many custom architectures.Highly reproducible and strong across modalities; essential baseline for brain MRI.Resource-intensive and not designed specifically for weak/limited labels.Central comparator for any proposed brain MRI segmentation method.A new method must outperform or complement nnU-Net, not just compare to weak baselines.
8Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsBrain tumor U-Net specializationBukhari, S. T., & Mohy-ud-Din, H. (2021/2022). E1D3 U-Net for Brain Tumor Segmentation: Submission to the RSNA-ASNR-MICCAI BraTS 2021 Challenge.Hierarchical brain tumor subregion segmentation: whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor.One-encoder, three-decoder 3D U-Net variant, with each decoder specialized for a tumor subregion.BraTS 2018 validation and BraTS 2021 challenge data.Comparable single-model performance without heavy ensembling.Simple task-specific specialization for tumor hierarchy.Still supervised and dependent on BraTS-style labels.Useful for discussing hierarchical target design in tumor segmentation.Would subregion-specific decoders benefit from SSL-pretrained shared encoders?
9Batch 1/2Area 1 — CNN/U-Net and Deep Learning FoundationsExplainable attention CNNGu, R., Wang, G., Song, T., et al. (2021). CA-Net: Comprehensive Attention Convolutional Neural Networks for Explainable Medical Image Segmentation. IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging.CNN segmentation lacks explainability and struggles with variation in position, scale, and shape.Comprehensive attention CNN combining spatial, channel, and scale attention.Multiple public medical image segmentation tasks; evaluated against attention/CNN baselines.Showed that multi-dimensional attention can improve accuracy and interpretability.Connects performance improvement with visual/explainable attention behavior.Not brain-MRI-specific and attention is not equivalent to causal explanation.Supports the survey discussion on explainable attention modules in clinical segmentation.Can attention outputs be validated clinically rather than visually inspected only?
10Batch 3Area 2 — Transformer and CNN–Transformer Brain MRI SegmentationCNN–Transformer brain tumor segmentationWang, W., Chen, C., Ding, M., Li, J., Yu, H., & Zha, S. (2021). TransBTS: Multimodal Brain Tumor Segmentation Using Transformer. MICCAI.CNNs capture local features but have limited long-range dependency modeling in 3D brain MRI.3D CNN encoder extracts volumetric features, Transformer models global dependencies, decoder recovers segmentation.BraTS 2019 and BraTS 2020 multimodal brain tumor MRI datasets.Reported comparable or higher performance than prior 3D state-of-the-art methods.Directly brain-MRI-specific and demonstrates CNN–Transformer complementarity.Heavy memory cost and supervised dependence on dense BraTS labels.Core transformer hybrid method in the survey taxonomy.How can transformer global context be retained under limited labels and deployment constraints?
11Batch 3Area 2 — Transformer and CNN–Transformer Brain MRI SegmentationPure transformer 3D segmentationHatamizadeh, A., Tang, Y., Nath, V., et al. (2022). UNETR: Transformers for 3D Medical Image Segmentation. WACV.CNN encoders have limited ability to learn global volumetric dependencies.Recasts 3D segmentation as sequence-to-sequence prediction with a ViT-style encoder and U-shaped decoder.BTCV multi-organ CT and MSD tasks including brain tumor segmentation.Reported strong performance on BTCV and MSD segmentation tasks.Foundational transformer architecture for volumetric medical segmentation.Data-hungry and computationally expensive compared with CNNs.Key paper for transformer-based segmentation subsection.Are pure transformer encoders practical for limited annotated brain MRI datasets?
12Batch 3/4Area 3 — Self-/Semi-Supervised Limited-Label LearningSelf-supervised transformer pretrainingTang, Y., Yang, D., Li, W., et al. (2022). Self-Supervised Pre-Training of Swin Transformers for 3D Medical Image Analysis. CVPR.Transformer-based 3D segmentation requires strong representations and large labeled datasets.Swin UNETR with self-supervised proxy tasks for 3D medical pretraining.Pretrained on 5,050 public CT images; fine-tuned on BTCV and MSD tasks.Demonstrated transfer benefits and strong leaderboard performance on segmentation tasks.Directly links SSL and transformer segmentation.Pretraining data mainly CT; transfer to brain MRI requires modality-specific validation.Important bridge between SSL and transformer segmentation for the proposed research.What SSL tasks are most appropriate for multimodal brain MRI rather than CT?
13Batch 3Area 2 — Transformer and CNN–Transformer Brain MRI Segmentation3D transformer segmentationZhou, H.-Y., Guo, J., Zhang, Y., Yu, L., Wang, L., & Yu, Y. (2023). nnFormer: Volumetric Medical Image Segmentation via a 3D Transformer. IEEE Transactions on Image Processing.Need stronger volumetric transformer designs that combine local and global representations.3D transformer with interleaved convolution/self-attention and local/global volume attention.Three public volumetric medical segmentation datasets.Reported substantial improvements over earlier transformer counterparts.Designed for volumetric context rather than adapting 2D transformers directly.Complex architecture and computational demands.Supports transformer taxonomy beyond TransBTS/UNETR.Which transformer components truly improve segmentation versus increasing complexity?
14Batch 3Area 2 — Transformer and CNN–Transformer Brain MRI SegmentationClinical-knowledge-guided multimodal transformerLin, J., Lin, J., Lu, C., et al. (2023). CKD-TransBTS: Clinical Knowledge-Driven Hybrid Transformer With Modality-Correlated Cross-Attention for Brain Tumor Segmentation. IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging.Naive multimodal MRI fusion may ignore radiological knowledge about modality roles.Dual-branch hybrid CNN–Transformer encoder with modality-correlated cross-attention and feature calibration.BraTS 2021 brain tumor segmentation challenge dataset.Reported state-of-the-art performance against CNN and transformer competitors.Clinically motivated multimodal fusion; highly relevant to brain tumor MRI.Architecture is complex and still relies on fully labeled BraTS supervision.Strongest survey example of clinical knowledge-guided multimodal transformer fusion.Can modality-aware fusion also handle missing modalities and incomplete clinical scans?
15Batch 4Area 3 — Self-/Semi-Supervised Limited-Label LearningUncertainty-aware teacher–student learningYu, L., Wang, S., Li, X., Fu, C.-W., & Heng, P.-A. (2019). Uncertainty-aware Self-ensembling Model for Semi-supervised 3D Left Atrium Segmentation. MICCAI.Voxel-wise medical annotation is expensive, making fully supervised segmentation difficult.Mean-teacher self-ensembling with uncertainty-guided consistency learning.2018 Left Atrium Segmentation Challenge 3D MR images.Improved performance by using unlabeled data while filtering unreliable teacher targets.Introduces uncertainty as a quality control mechanism for pseudo supervision.Validated on left atrium rather than brain tumor MRI.Foundational medical SSL method for limited-label 3D MRI segmentation.Can uncertainty gating reduce false pseudo-labels in heterogeneous brain tumors?
16Batch 4Area 3 — Self-/Semi-Supervised Limited-Label LearningShape-aware adversarial SSLLi, S., Zhang, C., & He, X. (2020). Shape-aware Semi-supervised 3D Semantic Segmentation for Medical Images. MICCAI.Semi-supervised segmentation can produce anatomically incomplete or implausible shapes.SASSNet jointly predicts segmentation and signed distance maps, using adversarial shape constraints.Atrial Segmentation Challenge dataset.Reported improved shape estimation over prior semi-supervised methods.Introduces geometric constraints for unlabeled medical data.Shape priors may be harder for irregular tumor morphology.Important for discussing geometry-aware SSL in medical segmentation.How can shape-aware SSL adapt to tumors whose shapes are pathologically irregular?
17Batch 4Area 3 — Self-/Semi-Supervised Limited-Label LearningDual-task consistencyLuo, X., Chen, J., Song, T., Chen, Y., Wang, G., & Zhang, S. (2021). Semi-supervised Medical Image Segmentation through Dual-task Consistency. AAAI.Conventional SSL often relies only on perturbation consistency and may ignore geometric task structure.Dual-task network predicts segmentation and level-set representation with consistency between the two tasks.Two public medical segmentation datasets.Reported performance gains over prior semi-supervised segmentation methods.Uses task-level regularization rather than only data/network perturbation.Additional task design may not transfer equally to all segmentation targets.Directly supports the survey gap on task-aware consistency for limited labels.Could dual-task consistency be combined with graph-level anatomical relationships?
18Batch 4Area 3 — Self-/Semi-Supervised Limited-Label LearningCNN–Transformer cross teachingLuo, X., Hu, M., Song, T., Wang, G., & Zhang, S. (2022). Semi-Supervised Medical Image Segmentation via Cross Teaching between CNN and Transformer. MIDL.Limited labels constrain both CNN and transformer segmentation models.CNN and transformer networks mutually teach each other using pseudo labels from unlabeled images.Public medical image segmentation benchmark; compared against eight SSL methods.Reported superior performance with a simple cross-teaching framework.Combines complementary CNN locality and transformer global context under SSL.Pseudo-label errors can be reinforced when both models are uncertain.Highly aligned with the proposed SSL + transformer/CNN direction.Can cross-teaching be extended to include graph models as a third relational teacher?
19Batch 4Area 3 — Self-/Semi-Supervised Limited-Label LearningMutual consistency learningWu, Y., Ge, Z., Zhang, D., Xu, M., Zhang, L., Xia, Y., & Cai, J. (2022). Mutual Consistency Learning for Semi-supervised Medical Image Segmentation. Medical Image Analysis.Limited annotations create uncertain and error-prone predictions in ambiguous medical regions.MC-Net+ uses shared encoder and multiple decoders with mutual consistency over soft pseudo labels.Three public medical segmentation datasets.Set a strong state of the art for semi-supervised medical segmentation in the reported settings.Explicitly targets hard/uncertain regions with multi-decoder disagreement.More decoders increase complexity and may need careful uncertainty calibration.Useful for proposed uncertainty-aware SSL segmentation pipeline.Can decoder disagreement identify hard tumor boundaries and missing-modality uncertainty?
20Batch 5Area 4 — Graph-Based, Dataset, Benchmark, and Evaluation FoundationsGraph attention brain tumor segmentationPatel, D., Patel, D., Saxena, R., & Akilan, T. (2023). Multi-class Brain Tumor Segmentation using Graph Attention Network.CNN/Transformer models may underuse explicit region-to-region relationships in volumetric MRI.Represents volumetric MRI as a region adjacency graph and applies graph attention networks.BraTS benchmark datasets including BraTS 2021 validation.Reported competitive Dice and HD95, with improvements over a GNN baseline.Directly brain-tumor-specific graph reasoning paper.Graph construction quality strongly affects performance; integration with CNN pipelines remains challenging.Core anchor for the survey’s relational/graph-based future direction.What graph nodes best represent tumor anatomy: voxels, supervoxels, regions, or modalities?
21Batch 5Area 4 — Graph-Based, Dataset, Benchmark, and Evaluation FoundationsVision graph U-NetJiang, J., Chen, X., Tian, G., & Liu, Y. (2023). ViG-UNet: Vision Graph Neural Networks for Medical Image Segmentation.CNNs model grid locality and transformers model patch sequences, but medical structures may need graph-like relationships.U-shaped encoder–decoder architecture with graph neural network blocks for image representation.ISIC 2016, ISIC 2017, and Kvasir-SEG medical segmentation datasets.Reported improvements over several U-shaped baselines.Shows how graph processing can be embedded inside a U-Net-like segmentation architecture.Not brain MRI; graph design must be adapted for 3D multimodal volumes.Supports graph-based architectural reasoning for the proposed brain MRI pipeline.How should 2D vision graph concepts be extended to 3D MRI and multimodal relationships?
22Batch 6Area 4 — Graph-Based, Dataset, Benchmark, and Evaluation FoundationsBrain tumor benchmark datasetMenze, B. H., Jakab, A., Bauer, S., et al. (2015). The Multimodal Brain Tumor Image Segmentation Benchmark (BRATS). IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging.Need standardized benchmark data and evaluation for brain tumor segmentation.Challenge framework with multimodal MR scans, expert annotations, simulated data, and standardized metrics.BRATS 2012/2013 benchmark with multi-contrast low- and high-grade glioma MRI.Established a benchmark that shaped the field of brain tumor segmentation.Standardized comparison, multimodal MRI, expert labeling.Challenge data may not reflect all real-world scanner/protocol variability.Primary benchmark foundation for the survey paper.How well do BraTS-trained models generalize to external hospitals and metastasis datasets?
23Batch 6Area 4 — Graph-Based, Dataset, Benchmark, and Evaluation FoundationsBrain metastasis datasetRudie, J. D., Weiss, D. A., Colby, J. B., et al. (2024). The University of California San Francisco Brain Metastases Stereotactic Radiosurgery MRI Dataset. Scientific Data.Brain metastasis segmentation lacks large, public, clinically realistic annotated MRI datasets.Public multimodal brain MRI dataset with expert voxelwise metastasis annotations and nnU-Net benchmarking.560 brain MRI examinations from 412 patients with 5,136 annotated brain metastases.Provides a clinically rich benchmark for small and multiple metastasis segmentation.Real clinical data, many lesions, useful for external validation beyond glioma.Single-institution origin and metastasis-specific imaging workflow.Supports missing-generalization and metastasis-relevance gaps in the survey.Can methods trained on glioma datasets handle small metastases and high lesion counts?
24Batch 6Area 4 — Graph-Based, Dataset, Benchmark, and Evaluation FoundationsBrain metastasis challenge benchmarkMoawad, A. W., Janas, A., Baid, U., et al. (2023/2024). The Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS-METS) Challenge 2023: Brain Metastasis Segmentation on Pre-treatment MRI.Need robust benchmarking for brain metastasis segmentation across international clinical datasets.Challenge dataset with stepwise annotations, lesion-wise Dice/HD95 evaluation, and false-positive/false-negative penalties.Eight international datasets; 1,303 studies annotated, with public training/validation/test splits.Identified small-lesion false negatives and mask misregistration as common failure modes.Clinically meaningful lesion-wise evaluation and diverse international data.Small lesions remain difficult; challenge ranking depends on evaluation design.Important for survey discussion of external validity and clinically relevant metrics.Should future brain MRI segmentation models optimize lesion-wise rather than only voxel-wise metrics?
25Batch 7Area 4 — Graph-Based, Dataset, Benchmark, and Evaluation FoundationsEvaluation metrics and validation rigorMaier-Hein, L., Reinke, A., Godau, P., et al. (2024). Metrics Reloaded: Recommendations for Image Analysis Validation. Nature Methods.Metric choice in biomedical image analysis often fails to match the clinical/scientific objective.Problem-aware framework for selecting complementary validation metrics.Methodological validation framework across biomedical image analysis tasks.Argues that poorly chosen metrics can misrepresent scientific progress and translation readiness.Provides principled guidance for Dice, Hausdorff, lesion-wise, and task-specific evaluation.Framework must be operationalized for each segmentation target and clinical context.Supports survey sections on evaluation, reproducibility, and clinical translation.Which metric set best evaluates small tumor detection, boundary accuracy, and clinical usability together?
