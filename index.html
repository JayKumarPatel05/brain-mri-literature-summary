1 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation Early CNN brain tumor segmentation Havaei, M., Davy, A., Warde-Farley, D., et al. (2017). Brain tumor segmentation with Deep Neural Networks. Medical Image Analysis.Open paper ↗ Manual glioma segmentation in multimodal MRI is slow, subjective, and difficult because tumors vary in size, shape, location, and contrast. Two-pathway CNN using local and global contextual features, a cascaded refinement design, and a two-phase training procedure to address class imbalance. BRATS 2013 glioblastoma MRI benchmark; reported state-of-the-art results while being substantially faster than earlier methods. Showed that tailored CNN architectures can outperform hand-crafted feature methods for brain tumor segmentation. Brain-MRI-specific; important early deep learning baseline; explicitly handles local/global context and label imbalance. 2D/patch-based design; limited volumetric context compared with modern 3D methods; less robust to heterogeneous clinical sites. Provides historical foundation for why deep learning replaced classical feature engineering in brain MRI segmentation. How much context is necessary for tumor subregion separation: local texture, global anatomy, or both?
2 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation Small-kernel CNN for brain tumors Pereira, S., Pinto, A., Alves, V., & Silva, C. A. (2016). Brain Tumor Segmentation Using Convolutional Neural Networks in MRI Images. IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging.Open paper ↗ Reliable automatic segmentation is challenging because brain tumors have strong spatial and structural variability across MRI patients. CNN segmentation architecture using small 3×3 kernels, intensity normalization, and data augmentation to reduce overfitting. BraTS benchmark MRI data; evaluated glioma tumor regions with Dice-based performance reporting. Demonstrated that deeper CNNs using small kernels can produce accurate tumor segmentation from MRI. Brain-tumor-specific; influential supervised CNN baseline; strong use of augmentation and normalization. Primarily supervised; does not address limited labels, missing modalities, or region-level structural reasoning. Supports the survey discussion of early CNN design choices for brain tumor segmentation. Can the same small-kernel strategy capture subtle boundaries when annotations are scarce or modalities are missing?
3 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation Multi-scale 3D brain lesion CNN Kamnitsas, K., Ledig, C., Newcombe, V. F. J., et al. (2017). Efficient Multi-Scale 3D CNN with Fully Connected CRF for Accurate Brain Lesion Segmentation. Medical Image Analysis.Open paper ↗ Brain lesions require both local detail and wider anatomical context for accurate segmentation across tumor, stroke, and injury tasks. Dual-pathway multi-scale 3D CNN with dense training and fully connected CRF post-processing. Brain lesion tasks including traumatic brain injury, brain tumors/BRATS, and ischemic stroke/ISLES; reported top-ranking challenge performance. Multi-scale 3D features improved lesion segmentation by combining local appearance and global context. Brain-specific; strong bridge from 2D CNNs to 3D lesion segmentation; uses multiple brain MRI lesion benchmarks. Patch-based inference and CRF post-processing increase pipeline complexity and computational burden. Directly supports brain MRI abnormality segmentation and the need for contextual modeling. Should modern systems retain explicit post-processing, or learn anatomical consistency end-to-end?
4 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation U-Net-based brain tumor FCN Dong, H., Yang, G., Liu, F., Mo, Y., & Guo, Y. (2017). Automatic Brain Tumor Detection and Segmentation Using U-Net Based Fully Convolutional Networks.Open paper ↗ Brain tumor extent must be delineated automatically and efficiently from 3D MRI volumes for treatment planning. U-Net-based fully convolutional network for automatic brain tumor detection and segmentation. BraTS 2015 MRI data with high-grade and low-grade glioma cases; evaluated through cross-validation. U-Net-based FCNs produced promising segmentation of tumor extent on multimodal MRI. Brain-tumor-specific U-Net adaptation; simple and relevant for baseline comparisons. Early U-Net-style implementation; limited compared with later 3D, transformer, and ensemble methods. Useful baseline for explaining how U-Net ideas migrated into brain tumor segmentation. What improvements are due to architecture versus preprocessing, augmentation, and training configuration?
5 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation BraTS 2017 U-Net/radiomics baseline Isensee, F., Kickingereder, P., Wick, W., Bendszus, M., & Maier-Hein, K. H. (2018). Brain Tumor Segmentation and Radiomics Survival Prediction: Contribution to the BRATS 2017 Challenge.Open paper ↗ Brain tumor segmentation and survival prediction require robust models that can handle class imbalance and multimodal MRI heterogeneity. Carefully modified U-Net with Dice loss, data augmentation, and radiomics-based survival prediction. BraTS 2015 and BraTS 2017 MRI data; reported strong Dice scores for whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor. A well-trained U-Net variant can achieve leading BraTS performance without excessive architectural novelty. Brain-MRI-specific; emphasizes training rigor, Dice loss, and augmentation. Still fully supervised and dependent on high-quality labeled BraTS data. Shows why strong baselines must be considered before proposing new architecture. Will a new SSL/graph method outperform a carefully tuned supervised U-Net under fair comparison?
6 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation Autoencoder-regularized 3D MRI segmentation Myronenko, A. (2018). 3D MRI Brain Tumor Segmentation Using Autoencoder Regularization.Open paper ↗ 3D tumor subregion segmentation needs robust volumetric features and regularization under limited labeled MRI data. Encoder–decoder 3D segmentation network with an auxiliary autoencoder reconstruction branch for regularization. BraTS 2018 multimodal brain MRI; winning/leading BraTS-style tumor subregion segmentation performance. Autoencoder regularization improved representation learning and robustness for 3D brain tumor segmentation. Brain-tumor-specific; strong volumetric model; connects segmentation with representation learning. Computationally heavy; fully supervised; reconstruction branch does not explicitly model region relationships. Important precursor to combining segmentation with self-supervised/reconstruction objectives. Can reconstruction pretext learning be made genuinely label-efficient rather than only auxiliary to supervised training?
7 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation Strong training baseline Isensee, F., Petersen, J., Klein, A., et al. (2018). No New-Net.Open paper ↗ Many BraTS improvements may come from better training protocols rather than increasingly complex architectures. Well-configured U-Net-style pipeline focused on preprocessing, training, patch size, Dice optimization, and augmentation. BraTS 2018 brain tumor segmentation challenge. A carefully trained U-Net baseline was highly competitive in BraTS 2018. Brain-MRI-specific challenge paper; excellent reminder to compare against strong baselines. Not designed for semi-supervised learning, missing modalities, or deployment constraints. Frames the committee question of whether proposed novelty adds value beyond strong engineering. What is the fair baseline: plain U-Net, No New-Net, nnU-Net, or a task-specific ensemble?
8 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation nnU-Net for brain tumor segmentation Isensee, F., Jaeger, P. F., Full, P. M., Vollmuth, P., & Maier-Hein, K. H. (2020). nnU-Net for Brain Tumor Segmentation.Open paper ↗ Brain tumor segmentation requires robust configuration across preprocessing, augmentation, architecture, and post-processing. BraTS-specific application of nnU-Net with region-based training, post-processing, aggressive augmentation, and pipeline modifications. BraTS 2020 multimodal brain MRI challenge. Task-specific nnU-Net modifications substantially improved brain tumor segmentation performance. Brain-MRI-specific; very strong baseline for any proposed method. Resource-intensive; relies on labeled data and extensive engineering. Should be treated as a minimum comparator for proposed segmentation work. Can SSL or graph reasoning improve generalization beyond what nnU-Net already achieves through engineering?
9 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation Multi-decoder U-Net Bukhari, S. T., & Mohy-ud-Din, H. (2021/2022). E1D3 U-Net for Brain Tumor Segmentation: Submission to the RSNA-ASNR-MICCAI BraTS 2021 Challenge.Open paper ↗ Hierarchical tumor subregions have different visual characteristics and may benefit from specialized decoding branches. One encoder with three decoder heads specialized for whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor. BraTS 2018 validation and BraTS 2021 challenge data. Specialized decoders improved subregion-oriented segmentation without excessive architectural complexity. Brain-tumor-specific; conceptually useful for subregion-specific modeling. Fully supervised; adds decoder complexity; depends on BraTS-style region definitions. Relevant to future graph nodes or region heads representing different tumor compartments. Should subregions be modeled as independent outputs, hierarchical labels, or connected graph nodes?
10 Area 1 — Deep Learning for Brain MRI Segmentation Multi-modal multi-scale attention fusion Aslam, W., Hussain, J., Aslam, M. Z., et al. (2025). Enhanced brain tumor segmentation in medical imaging using multi-modal multi-scale contextual aggregation and attention fusion. Scientific Reports.Open paper ↗ Brain tumor MRI segmentation must handle heterogeneous tumor shapes and complementary information across T1, T2, FLAIR, and T1-CE. Multi-modal multi-scale contextual aggregation with gated attention fusion to refine useful features and suppress noise. BraTS 2020 multimodal MRI; reported Dice values including 0.8158 for necrotic tumor regions and 0.8589 overall. Multi-scale context and attention fusion improved segmentation against several baseline architectures. Brain-MRI-specific; focuses on modality fusion and tumor heterogeneity. Recent single-study evidence; needs broader external validation and comparison with strong nnU-Net variants. Supports the proposed need for modality-aware feature learning in brain MRI. Do attention/fusion gains remain robust across institutions, scanners, and missing-modalities settings?
11 Area 2 — Edge AI / Real-Time Brain MRI Inference Fast whole-brain segmentation Roy, A. G., Conjeti, S., Navab, N., & Wachinger, C. (2019). QuickNAT: A Fully Convolutional Network for Quick and Accurate Segmentation of Neuroanatomy. NeuroImage.Open paper ↗ Whole-brain MRI segmentation is clinically useful but conventional pipelines can take hours, limiting real-time use. Fully convolutional densely connected network with auxiliary-label pretraining and manual-label fine-tuning. Eight structural brain MRI datasets across age, pathology, and scanners; segments MRI brain scans in roughly 20 seconds. QuickNAT achieved fast, accurate neuroanatomy segmentation, supporting real-time clinical feasibility. Directly brain MRI; strong fit for Edge AI/real-time inference concentration. Whole-brain anatomy rather than tumor-only segmentation; hardware/deployment constraints still require testing. Provides a brain-MRI-specific efficiency anchor without relying on generic mobile-vision papers. Can label-efficient SSL and graph reasoning be added without losing real-time inference speed?
12 Area 2 — Edge AI / Real-Time Brain MRI Inference 3D neuroanatomy segmentation Wachinger, C., Reuter, M., & Klein, T. (2018). DeepNAT: Deep Convolutional Neural Network for Segmenting Neuroanatomy. NeuroImage.Open paper ↗ Automatic segmentation of neuroanatomical brain structures from T1-weighted MRI is needed for quantitative analysis. Hierarchical 3D CNN with multi-task patch prediction, spatial coordinates, and CRF-based refinement. T1-weighted brain MRI neuroanatomy segmentation across 25 brain structures. DeepNAT compared favorably with state-of-the-art neuroanatomy segmentation methods. Brain-MRI-specific; combines CNN representation learning with anatomical spatial information. Patch-based and structure-focused; less directly targeted to tumor subregions or missing-modalities. Relevant for structural/anatomical priors and region-level brain modeling. Can anatomical priors improve tumor segmentation without over-constraining abnormal structures?
13 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation CNN–Transformer brain tumor segmentation Wang, W., Chen, C., Ding, M., Li, J., Yu, H., & Zha, S. (2021). TransBTS: Multimodal Brain Tumor Segmentation Using Transformer. MICCAI.Open paper ↗ CNNs capture local features but may struggle with long-range dependencies in 3D multimodal brain tumor MRI. 3D CNN encoder for local volumetric features, transformer bottleneck for global context, and decoder for dense segmentation. BraTS 2019 and BraTS 2020 multimodal brain tumor MRI datasets. Hybrid CNN–Transformer modeling achieved competitive or higher segmentation performance than prior 3D methods. Brain-MRI-specific; foundational CNN–Transformer paper for BraTS segmentation. Transformer component increases computational cost; requires labeled multimodal inputs. Core method anchor for transformer-based brain MRI segmentation in the survey. Can transformer context be retained while improving speed and low-label robustness?
14 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation Swin Transformer brain tumor segmentation Hatamizadeh, A., Nath, V., Tang, Y., Yang, D., Roth, H., & Xu, D. (2022). Swin UNETR: Swin Transformers for Semantic Segmentation of Brain Tumors in MRI Images.Open paper ↗ Brain tumors vary in size and spatial extent, requiring both local detail and long-range modeling in multimodal MRI. Swin Transformer encoder with U-shaped decoder and skip connections for 3D sequence-to-sequence tumor segmentation. BraTS 2021 brain tumor segmentation challenge. Ranked among top-performing approaches in BraTS 2021 validation. Brain-MRI-specific; directly links Swin Transformer hierarchy with U-Net decoding. High memory and training cost; still relies on labeled BraTS-style annotations. Important transformer baseline for the proposed brain MRI segmentation direction. Can Swin-based features support graph construction or SSL without excessive computational cost?
15 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation Self-supervised pretraining with CNN–Transformer ensembles Dobko, M., Kolinko, D.-I., Viniavskyi, O., & Yelisieiev, Y. (2021). Combining CNNs With Transformer for Multimodal 3D MRI Brain Tumor Segmentation With Self-Supervised Pretraining.Open paper ↗ BraTS segmentation needs accurate multimodal 3D models while benefiting from better initialization and representation learning. Modified TransBTS, nnU-Net, and ensemble strategy with self-supervised pretraining elements. BraTS 2021 validation set; reported Dice scores of 0.8496 ET, 0.8698 TC, and 0.9256 WT. Hybrid CNN–Transformer and nnU-Net ensembles can improve BraTS tumor subregion segmentation. Brain-MRI-specific; connects transformer segmentation with self-supervised pretraining. Ensemble complexity and compute cost may limit real-time deployment. Directly supports the survey’s SSL + transformer direction. Can similar gains be achieved with a single efficient model rather than an ensemble?
16 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation Incomplete multimodal transformer Zhang, Y., He, N., Yang, J., et al. (2022). mmFormer: Multimodal Medical Transformer for Incomplete Multimodal Learning of Brain Tumor Segmentation.Open paper ↗ Clinical brain MRI often lacks one or more modalities, causing performance drops in models trained on complete multimodal inputs. Hybrid modality-specific encoders, intra-modal and inter-modal transformer modules, and auxiliary regularizers for incomplete-modality robustness. BraTS 2018 brain tumor MRI; evaluated segmentation under many missing-modality combinations. Improved incomplete-modality segmentation, especially when only one modality is available. Brain-tumor-specific; directly addresses missing modalities, a key clinical gap. Complex architecture; requires systematic validation across external institutions. Essential for the survey’s missing-modality gap and proposed robust brain MRI direction. Should missing modalities be handled through synthesis, modality dropout, feature alignment, or graph-level fusion?
17 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation Clinical-knowledge-guided multimodal transformer Lin, J., Lin, J., Lu, C., et al. (2023). CKD-TransBTS: Clinical Knowledge-Driven Hybrid Transformer With Modality-Correlated Cross-Attention for Brain Tumor Segmentation. IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging.Open paper ↗ Naively concatenating MRI modalities may ignore how radiologists interpret modality relationships during brain tumor assessment. Dual-branch hybrid encoder with modality-correlated cross-attention and Trans&CNN feature calibration. BraTS 2021 brain tumor segmentation challenge; compared with CNN and transformer baselines. Achieved state-of-the-art brain tumor segmentation performance among compared methods. Brain-MRI-specific; clinically motivated multimodal fusion; strong survey anchor. Longer inference time; depends on modality grouping assumptions and complete multimodal data. Supports the proposed need for clinically meaningful feature fusion and modality reasoning. Can clinical-knowledge fusion be combined with SSL and graph structures for better low-label performance?
18 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation Pseudo-labeling for brain MRI Sari, C. T., Kurugol, S., Afacan, O., & Warfield, S. K. (2022). CORPS: Cost-free Rigorous Pseudo-labeling based on Similarity-ranking for Brain MRI Segmentation.Open paper ↗ Brain MRI segmentation suffers from limited labeled data, while unlabeled scans remain abundant. Atlas-based pseudo-labeling ordered by local intensity similarity, followed by 3D DCNN training on expert and pseudo-labeled scans. 3D brain MRI segmentation with labeled and unlabeled scans; focuses on binary segmentation of anatomical structures. Pseudo-labeling improved segmentation without additional manual labeling cost. Brain-MRI-specific; directly addresses label scarcity with pseudo-labeling. Pseudo-label quality depends on similarity ranking and atlas/label-fusion assumptions. Directly relevant to proposed pseudo-label generation and limited-label optimization. How can uncertainty filtering prevent poor pseudo-labels from propagating into downstream models?
19 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation Self-supervised missing-modality training Shen, Y., & Gao, M. (2019). Brain Tumor Segmentation on MRI with Missing Modalities.Open paper ↗ Clinical MRI exams may not contain all BraTS modalities, but most segmentation models expect complete multimodal input. Channel-independent encoding, feature-fusion decoding, channel dropout self-supervision, and feature-level domain adaptation. BraTS-style multimodal brain tumor MRI with simulated missing modalities. Segmentation quality depends on which modality is missing; modality contribution analysis aligns with clinical interpretation. Brain-tumor-specific; early and highly relevant missing-modality study. Older architecture; missingness is often simulated rather than fully clinical. Supports survey gaps around missing modalities and robust multimodal learning. Should missing-modal robustness be trained at image, feature, or graph-relation level?
20 Area 3 — Transformer, Semi-Supervised, and Missing-Modality Brain MRI Segmentation Semi-supervised brain metastasis segmentation Ottesen, J. A., et al. (2025). Semi-Supervised Learning Allows for Improved Segmentation of Brain Metastases.Open paper ↗ Brain metastasis segmentation requires expert voxelwise labels, but annotation is expensive and difficult for small lesions. Semi-supervised learning framework comparing supervised baselines with models trained using additional unlabeled brain MRI data. Brain metastasis MRI data with internal and external test evaluation. Semi-supervised learning improved segmentation over supervised baselines, especially in small-labeled-data and external-test settings. Brain-MRI-specific; directly supports label-efficient segmentation of small metastatic lesions. Metastasis-specific; needs comparison with broader glioma and multi-dataset settings. Strong support for the proposed low-label brain MRI segmentation direction. Can SSL reduce false negatives for very small lesions without increasing false positives?
21 Area 4 — Graph-Based, Benchmark Dataset, and Evaluation Foundations Graph attention brain tumor segmentation Patel, D., Patel, D., Saxena, R., & Akilan, T. (2023). Multi-class Brain Tumor Segmentation using Graph Attention Network.Open paper ↗ CNN and transformer models may underuse explicit region-to-region relationships in volumetric brain MRI. Constructs region adjacency graphs from MRI segmentation representations and applies graph attention networks for multiclass tumor segmentation. BraTS benchmark datasets including BraTS 2021 validation. Reported competitive Dice and HD95 results with improvement over a GNN baseline. Brain-tumor-specific; directly anchors the graph/relational modeling direction. Graph construction quality strongly affects performance; integration with end-to-end pipelines remains challenging. Core paper for the proposed SSL + segmentation + graph reasoning framework. What is the best graph node: voxel, supervoxel, tumor subregion, modality feature, or anatomical region?
22 Area 4 — Graph-Based, Benchmark Dataset, and Evaluation Foundations BraTS benchmark foundation Menze, B. H., Jakab, A., Bauer, S., et al. (2015). The Multimodal Brain Tumor Image Segmentation Benchmark (BRATS). IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging.Open paper ↗ Brain tumor segmentation needed standardized data and evaluation protocols to compare algorithms fairly. Challenge framework using multimodal brain MRI scans, expert labels, simulated data, and standardized metrics. BRATS 2012/2013 multi-contrast low-grade and high-grade glioma MRI. Established BraTS as a central benchmark for brain tumor segmentation research. Foundational brain-MRI benchmark; enables reproducible comparison. Challenge data cannot represent all scanner/protocol and clinical workflow variability. Primary dataset anchor for the survey and proposed brain MRI segmentation work. How well do BraTS-trained models generalize to metastasis, pediatric, and non-Western patient cohorts?
23 Area 4 — Graph-Based, Benchmark Dataset, and Evaluation Foundations BraTS 2021 benchmark Baid, U., Ghodasara, S., Mohan, S., et al. (2021). The RSNA-ASNR-MICCAI BraTS 2021 Benchmark on Brain Tumor Segmentation and Radiogenomic Classification.Open paper ↗ Brain tumor segmentation benchmarks needed larger multi-institutional data and clinically relevant molecular classification tasks. Large-scale challenge design for tumor subregion segmentation and MGMT promoter methylation classification. BraTS 2021 pre-operative baseline mpMRI from 2,040 patients. Expanded BraTS into a larger benchmark for segmentation and radiogenomic analysis. Large, multi-institutional, clinically relevant benchmark. Still challenge-curated; external clinical deployment requires additional validation. Provides modern benchmark context for comparing proposed algorithms. Should proposed models optimize only segmentation Dice, or also clinically relevant radiogenomic/downstream outcomes?
24 Area 4 — Graph-Based, Benchmark Dataset, and Evaluation Foundations Brain metastasis MRI dataset Rudie, J. D., Weiss, D. A., Colby, J. B., et al. (2024). The University of California San Francisco Brain Metastases Stereotactic Radiosurgery MRI Dataset. Scientific Data.Open paper ↗ Public datasets for brain metastasis segmentation are limited despite the clinical importance of detecting small and multiple lesions. Public multimodal brain MRI dataset with expert voxelwise metastasis annotations and nnU-Net benchmarking. 560 brain MRI examinations from 412 patients with 5,136 annotated brain metastases. Provided a large clinically rich benchmark for brain metastasis detection and segmentation. Brain-MRI-specific; many lesions; useful external validation beyond glioma. Single-institution dataset and metastasis-specific imaging workflow. Supports generalization testing beyond standard glioma BraTS data. Can a model trained on glioma data detect small metastases with acceptable lesion-wise sensitivity?
25 Area 4 — Graph-Based, Benchmark Dataset, and Evaluation Foundations Brain metastasis challenge benchmark Moawad, A. W., Janas, A., Baid, U., et al. (2023/2024). The Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS-METS) Challenge 2023: Brain Metastasis Segmentation on Pre-treatment MRI.Open paper ↗ Brain metastasis segmentation requires lesion-wise evaluation because small-lesion false negatives are clinically important. BraTS-METS challenge design with stepwise annotations, lesion-wise Dice/HD95, and false-positive/false-negative penalties. Eight international datasets with 1,303 studies and public train/validation/test splits. Highlighted small-lesion false negatives and registration/mask issues as key failure modes. Brain-MRI-specific; clinically meaningful lesion-wise evaluation. Segmentation remains difficult for small lesions; rankings depend on metric design. Guides evaluation criteria for clinically realistic brain MRI segmentation. Should future evaluation prioritize voxel overlap, lesion detection, boundary accuracy, or all three together?
